While early-stage startups rightly focus on product development and finding initial traction, neglecting unit economics can be a fatal mistake. Understanding the profitability of each customer relationship is crucial for building a sustainable business, not just a popular product.

### Key Metrics: CAC and LTV

At its core, unit economics revolves around two key metrics:

*   **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** How much does it cost you, on average, to acquire a new paying customer? Include all sales and marketing expenses.
*   **Lifetime Value (LTV):** How much total revenue (or profit, ideally) do you expect to generate from a single customer over the entire duration of their relationship with your company?

### The Golden Ratio: LTV > CAC

For a viable business model, your LTV must be significantly greater than your CAC. A common benchmark is an LTV:CAC ratio of 3:1 or higher. If it costs you more to acquire a customer than you'll ever make from them, your business is unsustainable.

### Why Track Early?

Even with limited data, estimating and tracking these metrics early helps you:

*   **Identify viable marketing channels:** Which channels bring in customers cost-effectively?
*   **Inform pricing strategy:** Is your pricing sufficient to cover CAC and generate profit?
*   **Assess scalability:** Can you acquire customers profitably as you grow?
*   **Attract investors:** Demonstrating positive unit economics (or a clear path to achieving them) is critical for fundraising.

Start tracking your unit economics today, even if the numbers are rough estimates. It forces critical thinking about your business model from day one.
